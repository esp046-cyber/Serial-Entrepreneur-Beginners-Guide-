EASIEST ₱500-2,000
Pros: Simple, cheap, full control
Cons: Unlimited personal liability
Best for: First-time entrepreneurs, service businesses

₱3,000-5,000
Pros: Shared capital & expertise

SCALABLE ₱15,000-30,000
Pros: Limited liability, easier funding, perpetual existence
Cons: Complex compliance, higher costs
Best for: Scaling businesses, investor-ready ventures

Step-by-Step Registration (2026 Streamlined Process):

STEP 1: Business Name Verification
DTI (Sole Prop) or SEC (Corp/Partnership)
• Check name availability online
• Reserve business name (₱200 DTI / ₱40 SEC)
• Valid for 30 days
Timeline: Same day (online)
🔗 bnrs.dti.gov.ph
STEP 2: Business Registration
DTI Registration (Sole Prop): ₱500
SEC Registration (Corp): ₱1,000 filing + 1% of authorized capital
• Submit requirements online via BRS
• Receive Certificate of Registration
Timeline: 1-3 days
STEP 3: Barangay Clearance
• Visit your barangay hall
• Submit business registration certificate
• Fee: ₱100-500 (varies per barangay)
Timeline: Same day
STEP 4: Mayor's Permit (Business Permit)
• Apply at City/Municipal Hall
• Submit: DTI/SEC cert, barangay clearance, lease contract
• Fee: ₱1,000-10,000+ (based on gross sales)
• 2026 UPDATE: Now available via Business One-Stop-Shop (BOSS)
Timeline: 1-3 days
STEP 5: BIR Registration (TIN & Taxes)
• Register for Tax Identification Number (TIN)
• Choose tax type: 8% (gross sales ≤₱3M) or graduated rates
• Register books of accounts
• Acquire Official Receipts/Invoices
Timeline: 1-5 days
🔗 bir.gov.ph
STEP 6: SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG
• SSS: Employer registration (₱0)
• PhilHealth: Business registration (₱0)
• Pag-IBIG: Employer registration (₱0)
• 2026: All can be done online
Timeline: 1-2 days each
⚡ Fast Track Option (2026):
Use the Business One-Stop-Shop (BOSS) system - complete registration in 1-3 days for ₱15,000-25,000 all-in (facilitated by accredited service providers).

🎯 Keys to Serial Entrepreneur Success ▼

The Filipino Serial Entrepreneur Playbook:

1. Start Lean, Scale Smart
• Launch MVP (Minimum Viable Product) in 30-60 days
• Test market with ₱10K-50K before big investment
• Use feedback to pivot quickly
• Scale only what's proven profitable
2. Build Systems, Not Jobs
• Document all processes (SOPs)
• Hire managers, not just workers
• Use automation tools (Zapier, Notion, Xero)
• Goal: Business runs without you in 6-12 months
3. Master Filipino Business Culture
• Build pakikisama with suppliers/partners
• Leverage utang na loob network (give first, receive later)
• Respect hierarchy but empower teams
• Balance formal contracts with relationship trust
4. Digital-First Mindset
• Online presence = credibility in 2026
• Invest in: Website, Google Business, social media
• Learn basics: SEO, FB Ads, email marketing
• Use data: Google Analytics, Meta Business Suite
5. Financial Discipline
• Separate personal & business accounts (DAY 1)
• Track every expense (use QuickBooks or Wave)
• Maintain 3-6 months emergency fund per business
• Reinvest 30-50% profits into growth
6. Network Strategically
• Join: PH Startup Summit, Founders Network PH, industry associations
• Attend: DTI SME events, chamber of commerce meetups
• Find mentor: Successful entrepreneurs 5-10 years ahead
• Give value first before asking for help
7. Know When to Exit
• Set exit goals before starting (IPO, acquisition, passive income)
• Build businesses others want to buy
• Typical serial entrepreneur cycle: 2-5 years per venture
• Exit options: Sell, franchise, hire CEO and become investor

⚠️ Common Pitfalls to Avoid ▼
❌ Starting Without Market Research
• Don't assume demand exists
• Survey 50-100 potential customers first
• Validate willingness to pay
• Study 3-5 competitors thoroughly
❌ Perfectionism Paralysis
• Don't wait for "perfect" conditions
• Launch with 80% ready product
• Improve based on customer feedback
• Filipino saying: "Walang perpekto, may pagkakataon lang"
❌ Underpricing Due to "Hiya"
• Don't undervalue your work to be "humble"
• Price based on value delivered, not hours
• Study competitor pricing
• Remember: Cheap = not trustworthy to many Filipinos
❌ Hiring Family/Friends Without Contracts
• Always have written agreements, even with relatives
• Set clear roles, responsibilities, compensation
• "Utang na loob" shouldn't mean free labor
• Protect relationships by being professional
❌ Ignoring Taxes & Compliance
• BIR penalties are severe (25-50% surcharge + interest)
• File quarterly returns even with zero income
• Keep all receipts for 3 years minimum
• Hire bookkeeper (₱5K-10K/mo) from day one
❌ Mixing Personal & Business Finances
• Separate bank accounts immediately
• Pay yourself a salary, don't just withdraw randomly
• Track personal loans to business separately
• Use business credit card for expenses
❌ No Written Contracts with Partners
• 60%+ of PH partnerships fail due to unclear agreements
• Define: Equity split, roles, decision-making, exit terms
• Use lawyers for ₱10K-30K (worth it!)
• Review annually as business evolves
❌ Copying Foreign Models Without Adaptation
• What works in US/SG may not work in PH
• Consider: purchasing power, internet speeds, payment preferences
• Filipinos prefer: Cash-on-delivery, installment, personal service
• Localize your approach
✅ The Serial Entrepreneur Advantage:
Learn from mistakes in Business #1, don't repeat in Business #2. Each venture should have 30-50% fewer errors than the last. By Business #3, you should have a proven playbook.

💰 Financial Management Mastery ▼

Cash Flow Management (The #1 Killer of Businesses)

⚠️ Critical Truth: 82% of Philippine small businesses fail due to poor cash flow management, NOT lack of profit. You can be profitable on paper and still go bankrupt!
Understanding Cash Flow
Cash Flow = Cash In (收入) - Cash Out (支出)
Cash In: Customer payments, loans, investments
Cash Out: Suppliers, salaries, rent, utilities, taxes

The Problem: You might sell ₱100K worth of products, but if customers pay in 30-60 days while you pay suppliers in 7 days, you're cash flow negative!
📊 Weekly Cash Flow Tracking (Do This Every Monday!)
Simple Spreadsheet Template:

Week Opening Balance Cash In Cash Out Closing Balance
Week 1 ₱50,000 ₱85,000 ₱60,000 ₱75,000
Week 2 ₱75,000 ₱90,000 ₱80,000 ₱85,000

Action Items:
• If closing balance drops 2 weeks in row → RED FLAG
• Maintain minimum 3 months operating expenses in bank
• Project next 12 weeks to anticipate problems
✅ Filipino Cash Flow Strategies:
1. Get Paid Faster
• Offer 5-10% discount for cash/immediate payment
• Accept GCash/Maya (instant settlement)
• Require 50% deposit for custom orders
• Bill immediately, follow up in 3 days

2. Pay Slower (Legally!)
• Negotiate 30-day terms with suppliers
• Use credit cards (30-45 day float)
• Time large payments after collections

3. Reduce Cash Needs
• Consignment with suppliers (pay only when sold)
• Dropshipping model
• Pre-sell products before ordering inventory
• Rent equipment vs buying

Pricing Strategies (Stop Underpricing!)

🔢 Cost-Plus Pricing (Beginner Method)
Selling Price = (Direct Costs + Overhead) ÷ (1 - Desired Profit %)
Example: Selling Cakes
• Direct costs: ₱150 (ingredients, packaging)
• Overhead per unit: ₱50 (rent, utilities, labor)
• Total cost: ₱200
• Desired profit: 40%
• Price = ₱200 ÷ (1 - 0.40) = ₱200 ÷ 0.60 = ₱333

Sell at ₱350 (round up for psychology)
💎 Value-Based Pricing (Advanced, Higher Margins)
Price based on customer value, NOT your costs!

Questions to ask:
• What problem are you solving?
• How much is that problem costing them?
• What would they pay for a solution?

Example: IT Support Service
• Your cost: ₱500/hour (salary, tools)
• Cost-plus price: ₱800/hour
• BUT: Client loses ₱50K if system is down
• VALUE price: ₱2,500-3,500/hour ✓

Works best for: Services, B2B, unique products, premium brands
🧠 Psychological Pricing for Filipino Market:
• ₱99, ₱499, ₱999 (perceived as cheaper than ₱100, ₱500, ₱1,000)
• Bundle pricing: "₱1,200 for 3" vs "₱450 each"
• Anchor high: Show ₱2,000 option first, then ₱1,200 feels like a deal
• Even numbers (₱500, ₱1,000) = quality, premium
• Odd numbers (₱499, ₱799) = bargain, sale
🚫 Pricing Mistakes Filipinos Make:
• Competing only on price (race to the bottom)
• Not raising prices for 2-3 years (inflation eats profit!)
• Giving too many discounts to friends/family
• Pricing same as competitors without differentiation
• Not tracking if profitable after ALL costs

Philippine Tax Optimization (Legal!)

📋 Tax Options for Small Businesses (2026)
Option 1: 8% Income Tax
BEST FOR MOST
• Gross sales ≤ ₱3,000,000/year
• No itemized deductions allowed
• Simple computation
• Example: ₱2M gross sales × 8% = ₱160K tax

Option 2: Graduated Income Tax
• Can deduct business expenses
• Better if high operating costs (40%+ of revenue)
• More paperwork, receipts required
• Rates: 0% up to ₱250K, then 20-35%

Which to choose?
• Low expenses business (digital, services) → 8%
• High expenses business (retail, manufacturing) → Graduated
✅ Legal Tax Deductions (Graduated Tax):
• Rent (office, store, warehouse)
• Employee salaries + benefits
• Utilities (electricity, water, internet)
• Office supplies & equipment
• Advertising & marketing
• Professional fees (lawyer, accountant)
• Vehicle expenses (if business use)
• Business meals (50% deductible)
• Training & seminars
• Depreciation of assets

Keep receipts for 3 years minimum!
Quarterly Tax Filing Calendar
Q1: January-March → File by April 15
Q2: April-June → File by July 15
Q3: July-September → File by October 15
Q4: October-December → File by January 15

Annual ITR: Due April 15 of following year

Set reminders! Late filing = 25% surcharge + 20% interest/year
💡 Tax Saving Tip:
Register spouse or family member as employee (legitimate work only!). You can deduct their salary + benefits, they file separate tax return with lower bracket. Legal if they actually work in business.

👥 Hiring & Philippine Labor Laws 101 ▼

When to Hire Your First Employee

🎯 Hire when you hit ANY of these:
• You're working 70+ hours/week consistently
• Turning down customers due to capacity
• Revenue > ₱200K/month and stable
• Spending time on ₱200/hour tasks (admin, basic tasks)
• Want to scale but you're the bottleneck
💼 Employment Types in Philippines
1. Regular Employee
MOST PROTECTIONS
• After 6 months probation → permanent
• Can only be fired for "just cause"
• Entitled to all benefits (13th month, leaves, SSS, etc.)
• Most expensive but stable workforce

2. Project-Based
• Fixed duration (e.g., 3 months project)
• Must be for specific project/task
• Entitled to pro-rated 13th month
• Contract ends when project ends

3. Contractual (through Agency)
• Hired through manpower agency
• Agency handles payroll, benefits
• More expensive (agency fee 10-20%)
• Easier to terminate

4. Part-Time
• < 8 hours/day or < 40 hours/week

5. Independent Contractor
CAREFUL!

Mandatory Benefits (You MUST Provide)

1. 13th Month Pay
What: Additional month salary paid in December
Computation: Total basic salary for the year ÷ 12
Deadline: December 24
Who: ALL employees (even those who worked <1 year, pro-rated)

Example:
• Monthly salary: ₱18,000
• Started: July 1 (worked 6 months)
• 13th month: (₱18,000 × 6) ÷ 12 = ₱9,000
2. SSS (Social Security System)
Purpose: Retirement, disability, death, maternity benefits
Contribution: 14% of monthly salary
• Employer: 9.5%
• Employee: 4.5%
Max salary base: ₱30,000
Due: Every month by 10th

Example (₱20,000 salary):
• Employer pays: ₱1,900
• Employee pays: ₱900 (deducted from salary)
• Total to SSS: ₱2,800/month

Purpose: Health insurance
Contribution: 5% of monthly salary (shared 50-50)
• Employer: 2.5%
• Employee: 2.5%
Due: Monthly by 10th

• Employee pays: ₱500
• Total: ₱1,000/month

Purpose: Housing fund, savings, loans
Contribution:
• Employee: 1-2% of monthly salary
• Employer: 2% of monthly salary
Due: Monthly by 10th

Example (₱20,000 salary):
• Employer pays: ₱400

Termination & Separation

⚠️ You CANNOT Just Fire Someone in PH!
The Philippines has strong employee protections. Illegal termination = reinstatement + backwages + damages (can cost ₱500K-2M+)
Legal Grounds for Termination
Just Causes (Serious Misconduct):
• Theft, fraud, dishonesty
• Serious misconduct
• Willful disobedience
• Gross neglect of duty
• Loss of trust (for positions with fiduciary role)
• Crime against employer/family

Process Required:
1. Written notice specifying cause
2. Hearing/opportunity to explain
3. Written notice of decision

Separation Pay: NONE (for just cause)
Authorized Causes (Business Reasons)
• Redundancy
• Retrenchment (to prevent losses)
• Closure of business
• Disease (if continued employment is risky)

Requirements:
• 30-day written notice to employee & DOLE
• Separation pay required

Separation Pay:
• 1 month salary OR
• ½ month pay per year of service
• Whichever is HIGHER
✅ Best Practice: Probation Period Strategy
• Set 6-month probation (standard)
• Give clear performance standards in writing
• Evaluate at 3 months, 5 months
• If not meeting standards, end before 6 months
• After 6 months = regular = very hard to terminate

During probation: Can end employment with 30-day notice, no separation pay needed

💰 Food Costing Formula

• Vegetables: ₱10
• Sauce & spices: ₱7
• Packaging: ₱5

₱55 ÷ 0.30 = ₱183 × 1.12 = ₱205
Sell at ₱199-209

• Food cost: ₱55 (30%)

🛠️ Service Business

💼 Service Pricing Strategy
Hourly vs Project vs Retainer:

Hourly Pricing
• Formula: (Desired annual income + overhead) ÷ billable hours
• Example: ₱720K/year ÷ 1,000 hours = ₱720/hour
• Pros: Simple, fair for variable work
• Cons: Penalizes efficiency, income capped

Project Pricing
• Estimate hours × rate × 1.5 (complexity buffer)
• Example: 10 hours × ₱720 × 1.5 = ₱10,800
• Pros: Predictable for client, rewards speed
• Cons: Risky if scope creeps

Retainer Model (BEST)
• Monthly fee for ongoing services
• Example: ₱15K-50K/month for social media management
• Pros: Predictable income, client retention
• Cons: Must deliver consistent value
